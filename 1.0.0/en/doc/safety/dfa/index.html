    neXtBSW dependent failure analysis of the stage-1 mechanisms¶

    Baseline: v0.2.0

    Status: 2026-09-28, issue 1, written against dev at ae92e89. Update 2026-10-03 (implementation of the findings): DFA-1, DFA-2, DFA-3, DFA-4 (a) and DFA-6 are implemented in the family port and host verified, DFA-5 and AoU-DFA-a … AoU-DFA-c are in the safety manual; the rows DF-05, DF-14, DF-36, DF-46 and DF-47 changed their status, section 0 counts them, section 8.1 says what was done and what stays open, and the consistency script of section 10.2 is checked in as test/test_dfa.py. Update 2026-10-04 (board results of a separate task of 2026-10-03/04 and their safety review of 2026-10-04, all points as recommended): DF-05 from covered (host) to partial - a gated LPIT clock is a bus fault caught by SM-17, a slow LPIT clock is caught by DFA-2 on the board, a lost SIRC is a residual risk carried by an earlier change; DFA-6 and section 8.1 corrected: a stopped system time is not kind 4 (SM-06 and SM-01 react), kind 4/5 see a slow or fast one. Readers: the architect and safety owner, the maintainer, the contributors of a separate task (freedom from interference), device packages, hardware-software interface, ECC latent test and MCU safety manual (safety manual). Language: English, like the rest of doc/.

    What this document is. The dependent failure analysis (DFA) that the gap analysis iso26262-gap.md (not published, available on request) section 6 no. 15 asks for (rows 9-03, 9-04, 9-05, 9-11, 6-16, 10-07 and 11-02 there): the stage-1 safety mechanisms of neXtBSW run on one MCU, one core, one supply and one die together with the software they supervise. This document asks, systematically and per topic, which single cause can make a mechanism fail together with the element it supervises or together with another mechanism that is supposed to catch the same fault (common cause), and which failure of one element can propagate into a mechanism (cascading failure). For every potential dependent failure it states whether it is plausible, which measure exists or is proposed, and why that measure is adequate. The SPI common cause (finding F5 of fmea.md) is analysed explicitly (section 6).

    Licence rules followed here. ISO 26262 is not in the repository and was not available to this analysis (contributor session, doc/confidential/ does not exist in the remote repository). The standard is cited by part and clause only, as the gap analysis does, and no text or table of it is reproduced. The topic list of section 3 is the grouping the gap analysis gives for 9-7 (row 9-03), completed with the software topics that 6-7.4.11 and the freedom-from-interference annex of part 6 name, all in our own words. Whether the list is complete against the clause and its informative annex is open point O1 (section 10): it needs a reader of the licensed copy.

    What it is not. Not a quantitative analysis (no beta factors, no failure rates — those are the integrator's, AoU-09), not the DFA of the device packages, and not the freedom-from-interference design itself. It is the list that tells which dependent failures they have to close or to accept, and why the ones that are already closed are.

    1. Method¶

    1.1 Terms¶

    In our own words:

    • Dependent failure — two or more failures whose probability of occurring together is higher than the product of their single probabilities. Two kinds:
    • Common cause failure (CCF) — one root cause makes several elements fail, directly, through a coupling factor they share (the same clock, the same supply, the same code origin, the same person);
    • Cascading failure (CF) — the failure of one element causes the failure of another (a runaway task corrupts the supervisor's data; a stuck SPI transfer stalls a task).
    • Initiator — the event or fault that starts the dependent failure.
    • Coupling factor — the shared property or resource through which it spreads.
    • Independence vs. freedom from interference. Freedom from interference (FFI) is the absence of cascading failures from a lower-rated element into a higher-rated one. Independence additionally excludes common causes. At ASIL B neXtBSW does not use decomposition (gap 9-01), so it needs independence only where the argument relies on it: where a mechanism is claimed to detect a fault of the element it supervises, or to cover the latent faults of another mechanism. That is the list of section 2.

    1.2 Steps¶

    1. What must be independent (section 2): from fmea.md section 3 and concept.md section 6, every pair "supervised element ↔ mechanism", "mechanism ↔ mechanism that covers its latent faults" and "detection ↔ reaction path" that the stage-1 argument relies on.
    2. Shared resources (section 4): for every mechanism, what it needs to work — hardware on the die, execution context, code and data, tools, people. A resource used by both sides of a pair of section 2 is a coupling factor.
    3. Topics (section 3): each topic is walked against the shared resources to find initiators.
    4. Per potential dependent failure (section 5): kind (CCF/CF), plausibility with reason, measure, rationale, status, evidence. A measure is one of three types, in the order of preference the gap analysis gives for 9-7:
    5. P — prevents the root cause (for example the CRC unit reserved for one user);
    6. D — detects or controls the effect (for example a check on an independent clock);
    7. R — reduces the coupling (for example a separate time base, a separate execution context).
    8. Findings and hand-over (sections 7 and 8): what is not closed becomes a finding, an addition to an existing task, or an assumption of use.

    1.3 Status words¶

    Status Meaning
    covered a measure exists in the code and is verified (host tests with built-in faults and/or board, as named)
    covered (host) the measure exists and is host verified; the board proof is open (named)
    partial a measure exists but leaves a named gap
    open no measure yet; a finding DFA-n (section 8) or an addition to a named task
    AoU the element cannot close it; the integrator's duty, as a proposed assumption of use (section 9)
    residual accepted, with the reason and who accepted it
    not plausible the dependent failure cannot occur in the stage-1 design, with the reason

    1.4 Plausibility¶

    A potential dependent failure is plausible if the initiator can occur in the target environment of the element (an S32K1 ECU in a vehicle, concept 1.2) and the coupling factor exists in the code at ae92e89. It is not plausible if the coupling factor does not exist (for example no second user of a resource) or if the initiator is excluded by construction (for example a build error). Rates are not estimated (concept section 0, AoU-09).

    6. SPI as a common cause (F5)¶

    fmea.md section 5.3 decided that the SPI master is not a stage-1 problem. This analysis confirms the decision with the dependent-failure view and names what stays open for the other tasks.

    Stage 1 does not depend on SPI. None of the mechanisms of section 4 uses R-19. The safe outputs walk writes port and FTM registers; the gate-driver enable is a GPIO declared safe_off (TMC6200 drv_en), so S1 takes effect without any SPI transfer. The only SPI-dependent function near a stage-1 mechanism is the SBC watchdog trigger, and its failure ends in an SBC reset — a safe direction (stage 2, SM-42).

    No SPI failure can undo S1. A wrong command to the gate driver (wrong chip select, corrupted frame, a QM task writing the device) cannot energise the bridge while DRV_EN is held inactive by the table walk, because the enable does not go over SPI. That is the property that makes the SPI a stage-2 common cause and not a stage-1 one; it holds as long as every power stage has a safe_off enable outside the bus (AoU-06, and DFA-4 for the PWM side).

    9. Proposed assumptions of use¶

    Numbers are assigned by the maintainer (AoU-24/25 are proposed in fmea.md, AoU-26 … AoU-29 in tool-classification.md, AoU-30 is in the concept; other tasks of the same wave may propose more).

    ID Assumption
    AoU-DFA-a The integrator confirms that the MCU's own power-on and low-voltage reset (or an external supply supervisor) brings the MCU into reset before its supply leaves the range in which the MCU manufacturer guarantees correct operation. neXtBSW's supply check (SM-20) runs on the same die and is credited only for a drift while the die still works correctly (DF-01).
    AoU-DFA-b The stage-1 mechanisms detect faults that stop, divert or delay the program flow, or raise an exception, on an independent time base. They do not detect a core fault that computes wrong results on a correct program flow (DF-06). If the integrator's hardware metrics need that coverage, he provides it — a core self-test required by the MCU manufacturer's safety manual, an external challenge-response watchdog (AoU-13) or a second channel.
    AoU-DFA-c The run-time register, MPU and safe-outputs checks compare with the configured values: they detect a change after start, not a wrong configuration (DFA-5). The integrator verifies the configuration itself — inactive levels, safe_off declarations, dead times, MPU regions — against his design and on his hardware (AoU-06, AoU-07, AoU-10).

    11. Review questions of the architecture (section 11)¶

    This task changed no code, no generator, no configuration and no bootloader: one new document. The questions are answered for the analysis and for the proposed measures.

    1. Board without an SBC? Yes. No stage-1 measure relies on the SBC (DF-35); the independence the analysis credits comes from three on-chip clocks. The loss of independence from the die without an external watchdog is stated (AoU-13, AoU-DFA-b).
    2. Different crystal and system clock? Yes. The clock-error band of DF-02 is stated relative to the nominal period, not to 80 MHz; DFA-6 (a) and DFA-2 compare against the LPO, not against a frequency constant.
    3. Different derivative, S32K3 / STM32 / Cortex-R? Yes. The analysis is by resource class (supply, clocks, memories, timers); the S32K1 registers are named as the instance of the family port. Another family repeats the resource list of section 4 with its own peripherals; DFA-2 and DFA-3 are family-port measures.
    4. Two or more CAN buses? Yes. DF-32 and SPI-1 are per bus; SM-32 and SM-34 are per bus instance.
    5. Several tasks and free priorities? Yes; DF-36, DF-37, DF-43 and DFA-1 are exactly about what a task set can do to the mechanisms. The only fixed rule used is above_application for the safety task, which the configuration already checks.
    6. Every new value in one configuration level? No new value. DFA-1's deadline is derived from the existing module parameter modules.safeMemory.rom_check_period_ms; DFA-6 (a) derives its bound from the safety task period and the LPO range already in the configuration.
    7. Host test with its own test profile? Each proposed measure names its host test with built-in faults. The document itself is checked by the consistency script (section 10.2).
    8. Tool core free of Qt, messages with a YAML path? DFA-4 (a) is an nxconfig warning with the YAML path of the PWM group.
    9. Device driver granularity? Not applicable — no driver changed; the SPI part only hands SPI-1/SPI-2 to a separate task.
