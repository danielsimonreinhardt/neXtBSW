    1. Structure of the case¶

    1.1 Notation¶

    The case is written as tables so that a test can check it; the structure follows the usual claim–argument–evidence pattern:

    • Claim (C-nn, section 2): a statement that the element is meant to meet. For an SEooC the claims are the assumed requirements (2-6.4.5.7, 10-9.2.4): the assumed safety goals of concept.md section 2.2 and the claim of the safety plan section 1.2.
    • Argument (A-nn, A-P<part>, sections 3 and 4): the strategy that says why the claim holds, the basis it decomposes into (requirements, assumptions of use, activities, sub-claims), the evidence it cites, the deficits it admits and a verdict.
    • Evidence (E?-nn, section 5): one row per item, with the reference the test resolves, since which baseline it exists and its status.
    • Deficit (D-nn, section 6): something the argument needs and does not have — the counter-evidence of the case. Every deficit names the task that removes it, or the tailoring of the safety plan that hands it on.

    Verdicts, from strong to weak: supported, supported with limits, partly supported, not supported. An argument is supported only without deficits and with all its evidence available; a claim is never stronger than what concept.md section 2.2 says about its coverage; the top claim is never stronger than its weakest sub-claim (all checked).
